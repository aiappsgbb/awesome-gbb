# Evidence and claims

Evidence constrains the message; it is not decoration added after choosing a story.

## Establish provenance

Use supplied material first. For every consequential, externally verifiable
factual claim, inspect the actual source and record a locator: URL and section,
file and page, dataset and field, or authorized internal record. A search snippet
or plausible URL alone is not verification.

Keep this information in the existing storyboard's source register. Distinguish:

| State | How to use it |
|---|---|
| Verified fact | State within the source's population, period and scope |
| Source-reported claim | Attribute it; reporting is not independent confirmation |
| Interpretation | Explain the facts and reasoning from which it follows |
| Estimate or hypothesis | Label it and identify assumptions or the needed test |
| Synthetic illustration | Label it where shown; never imply a measured result |
| Missing or conflicting evidence | Expose the gap and qualify, remove or defer the claim |

Look for contradictory evidence and freshness problems. Compare definitions and
measurement conditions before reconciling conflicting numbers. Do not average
incompatible studies or choose the more persuasive figure. If only an abstract
was accessible, say so rather than claiming to have read the full paper.

Use additional research for identified gaps. Never send confidential documents,
customer details or private claims to public search or external generators.
Respect source permissions and publication scope. Summarize methods in original
words with attribution; do not copy proprietary training materials.

## Numbers and comparisons

For a material number retain its unit, denominator or population, period,
baseline, method and source. Distinguish observed values from projections.
For a derived number retain the formula and input locators so it can be checked.
Use precision justified by the inputs.

For example, a **synthetic** change from 40 minutes to 30 minutes is a 25%
reduction in elapsed time: `(40 - 30) / 40`. It is not a 25-percentage-point
change, nor automatically a 25% cost saving. Converting time to money needs
additional assumptions about volume, labor and whether capacity is actually saved.

Compare benchmarks only when workloads, versions, hardware, metrics and test
conditions support the comparison. A vendor's best-case result is not the
customer's expected outcome. An integration example is not a performance test.
Correlation, before/after change and controlled causal evidence are different.

Choose chart scales and baselines that do not exaggerate the result; make any
nonzero scale or transformed axis clear. A chart caption or subtitle should
identify measure, unit, population and period. Preserve uncertainty or ranges
when they affect interpretation. Do not infer exact numbers from a decorative
graphic or redraw approximate figures as precise measurements.

## Implications and limits

Ask what the evidence actually permits the audience to conclude. Does the
recommendation require an extra assumption? What would change it?

Put a qualification beside the claim when omitting it would mislead. A source
link in notes does not repair an overstated headline. If a source cannot be
accessed, preserve the locator and mark the claim unverified. Do not substitute
an invented citation, silently promote it to a fact, or describe the deck as
fully verified.
